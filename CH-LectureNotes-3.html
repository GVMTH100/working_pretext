    Worksheet Order of operations
    

    The Order of Operations is an agreed upon order in which operations must be completed when simplifying a numeric or an algebraic expression. When presented as a list of rules, the Order of Operations can seem arbitrary. However, there are mathematical reasons underlying the order in which operations are completed. In general we preform more complicated operations first and then work down in terms of complexity.
    🔗
    Our simplest operation is addition which is undone by subtraction. Next, we consider multiplication which we consider repeated additions. For example, \(3\cdot4\) can be thought of as adding 4 together 3 times or \(3\cdot4=4+4+4\text{.}\) Multiplication is undone with division, so they are treated as the same level of complexity. Next, we consider exponents which can be considered repeated multiplications. For example, \(3^4\) can be thought as multiplying 3 by itself 4 times, that is \(3^4=3\cdot3\cdot3\cdot3.\) Finally, if we want to alter the order we use a grouping symbol, generally parentheses or fraction bars in this course. So our order of operation follows:
    🔗
    G Grouping symbols, including parentheses and fraction bars
    E Exponents and Radicals (left to right)
    M Multiplication and Division (left to right)
    A Addition and Subtraction (left to right)
